Why this matters before MT5 development

Many strategy descriptions mix objective rules with visual judgment. A developer cannot reliably code phrases such as “strong candle”, “good support”, “clean breakout” or “enter when momentum looks right” unless each one is converted into measurable conditions. A structured brief makes those ambiguities visible early.

What a complete EA specification should cover

  • Signal definition: exact indicator, price-action or structure conditions and the order in which they must occur.
  • State: whether a setup remains valid for one candle, several bars, until invalidated, or until a session ends.
  • Execution: market versus pending orders, maximum spread, slippage behavior and retry rules.
  • Risk: fixed volume or percentage risk, total portfolio exposure and daily loss limits.
  • Management: partial closes, break-even, trailing stops, pyramiding and opposite-signal behavior.
  • Failure behavior: what the EA does when the broker rejects an order, data is missing or the terminal reconnects.
  • Validation: data period, metrics and acceptance criteria for backtest and forward test.
